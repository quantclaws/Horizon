## [Latent Flow Matching Generates Arbitrage-Free Implied Volatility Surfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00616) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces a latent flow-matching framework that first compresses implied volatility surfaces into a low-dimensional latent space using a VAE regularized by differentiable calendar-spread, call-spread, and butterfly-arbitrage penalties, then learns a flow-matching model to transport a Gaussian prior to the empirical latent distribution, and decodes generated latent samples back into full volatility surfaces. Generating arbitrage-free volatility surfaces is crucial for realistic option pricing and risk management; this method avoids costly post‑hoc reweighting and directly produces financially consistent surfaces, offering a practical tool for quantitative finance and ML‑driven market modeling. The VAE encoder is penalized with differentiable approximations of calendar‑spread, call‑spread and butterfly arbitrage conditions; a flow‑matching model learns optimal transport in the latent space, and evaluation shows the model reproduces the empirical distribution, excels in the extreme Q99 tail, and yields 90.8% of generated surfaces satisfying all static no‑arbitrage tests.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Aug 4, 04:00

**Background**: Latent flow matching applies flow‑matching generative models in the compressed latent space of an autoencoder, improving efficiency for high‑dimensional data such as volatility surfaces. Implied volatility surfaces encode market expectations across strikes and maturities, and must satisfy no‑arbitrage constraints like calendar‑spread, call‑spread and butterfly conditions to be financially valid. Variational autoencoders (VAEs) learn probabilistic encoders and decoders, and can be regularized with differentiable penalty terms to enforce constraints during training.

## [ML-driven HFT measures distinguish liquidity supply and demand, improve price informativeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00858) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers introduced data-driven machine learning measures that separate liquidity-supplying and liquidity-demanding high-frequency trading strategies, training models on a proprietary HFT dataset and applying them to public intraday data for all U.S. stocks from 2010 to 2023. The resulting measures outperform traditional proxies, respond to quasi-exogenous events such as a speed bump introduction and a data feed upgrade, and reveal that liquidity-supplying HFT improves price informativeness around earnings announcements while liquidity-demanding HFT impedes it. These measures provide a more accurate tool for assessing high-frequency trading activity, enabling researchers, regulators, and market participants to better understand how different HFT strategies affect market quality and price informativeness. By distinguishing liquidity supply from demand, the work clarifies the mixed effects of HFT on market liquidity and informs policy debates about market structure reforms. The machine learning models were trained on a proprietary dataset with observed HFT activity and then applied to public intraday data covering all U.S. equities from 2010 through 2023. The measures outperform conventional proxies, show significant responses to a quasi‑exogenous speed bump introduction and a data feed upgrade, and reveal that liquidity‑supplying HFT improves price informativeness around earnings announcements whereas liquidity‑demanding HFT reduces it.

**Background**: High-frequency trading (HFT) refers to algorithmic trading strategies that execute large numbers of orders at extremely high speeds, often to capture small price discrepancies. ## [Projection‑Consistent Neural Operator Accelerates Local‑Stochastic Volatility Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01217) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces a projection‑consistent neural operator (implemented with DeepONet or FNO) that jointly learns the implied‑volatility surface, Dupire local volatility, LSV leverage and conditional moment while enforcing static‑arbitrage and projection constraints. In synthetic experiments it reduces calibration latency from 98.5 ms to 0.6 ms and improves local‑volatility RMSE by 36% and leverage RMSE by 7‑16%. By amortizing the expensive McKean–Vlasov fixed‑point solve into a single operator evaluation, the method removes a major computational bottleneck in quantitative‑finance model calibration, enabling faster pricing and risk‑management workflows. The theoretical guarantees of identification and consistency also provide a principled foundation for deploying neural operators in other inverse problems. The method implements DeepONet and FNO to enforce quote fit, static‑arbitrage, Dupire and projection constraints, using a division‑free Dupire residual in log‑implied‑variance and a quotient Fokker–Planck equation after Gyöngy projection. Under LSV existence and inverse residual stability it proves conditional identification and empirical consistency, and in synthetic tests achieves 0.1‑0.2 pp error vs. particle methods, reduces latency from 98.5 ms to 0.6 ms and improves local‑volatility RMSE by 36% (leverage RMSE by 7‑16%).

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Aug 4, 04:00

**Background**: Local‑stochastic volatility (LSV) models combine the marginal distributions of vanilla options with a stochastic‑volatility backbone to capture richer smile dynamics, but their calibration requires solving a slow, noisy McKean–Vlasov fixed‑point problem. Gyöngy’s theorem shows that the local volatility component can be viewed as a Markovian projection of the underlying stochastic dynamics, which allows the calibration to be expressed as a projection‑consistent operator. Neural operators such as DeepONet and FNO learn mappings between function spaces that are invariant to discretization, making them suitable for amortizing expensive fixed‑point solves into rapid online evaluations.

## [LLMs Boost Product Idea Quality but Reduce Novelty and Diversity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27553) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The study shows that LLM-generated product ideas for college students under $50 have higher average quality (measured by purchase intent) and are seven times more likely to rank in the top 10%, yet they are less novel at the idea level and less diverse at the set level compared to human-generated ideas. Understanding this trade‑off helps innovation managers leverage LLMs for quality while applying proven techniques—such as cross‑vendor pooling, prompt engineering, and scaling idea generation—to recover novelty and diversity. The researchers found that newer LLM vendors and versions produce more diverse ideas, but still below human levels; diversity can be boosted by pooling ideas across vendors, using Chain‑of‑Thought prompts, injecting heterogeneous personas or constraints, and deploying creative agents that explore the solution landscape, while scaling the number of ideas improves coverage of the idea space toward human levels.
