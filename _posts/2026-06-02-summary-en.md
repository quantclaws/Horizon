## [What appear to be biochemical processes may be a natural feature of geology](https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-dirt-that-refused-to-die-20260601/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers report that geological processes such as serpentinization at hydrothermal vents can generate organic molecules that resemble biochemical compounds, indicating that life‑like chemistry can arise abiotically. This finding strengthens the hypothesis that life’s chemistry could emerge from geology alone, with implications for origin‑of‑life studies and the search for life on icy moons like Europa and Enceladus. The abiotic synthesis occurs via serpentinization, where water reacting with olivine produces hydrogen and reduces carbon to form simple organics, though aromatic amino acids remain unlikely to form abiotically.

hackernews · speckx · Jun 1, 15:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48357905)

**Background**: Serpentinization is a geological reaction in which seawater percolates into ultramafic rocks, transforming olivine into serpentine minerals while releasing hydrogen gas and creating alkaline, reducing environments conducive to abiotic organic synthesis. Hydrothermal vents, especially alkaline vents like Lost City, provide long‑lived chemical gradients that can drive the formation of molecules such as methane, formate, and simple amino acids without biological enzymes. These processes are considered plausible pathways for the emergence of life on early Earth and are now being evaluated as potential energy sources for life in subsurface oceans of icy moons such as Europa.

## [Measuring Local Network Effects Across Major Social Media Platforms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04545) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Using incentive-compatible online choice experiments with 19,923 US users of Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and X, the study measured local network effects and found platform value per user ranges from $78 to $101 per month, with 8.1‑23.7% of that value attributable to local ties. It provides the first large‑scale empirical measurement of local network effects across major platforms, revealing how tie strength and connection value differ by platform, user demographics, and cross‑platform usage, which is crucial for understanding digital economies and guiding platform design. Stronger ties are more valuable on Facebook and Instagram, while weaker ties are more valuable on LinkedIn and X; work connections are most valuable on LinkedIn and least on Facebook; men value connections to women significantly more than to other men, especially on Instagram, Facebook, and X; white users disproportionately value same‑race ties on Facebook, and Instagram users value ties to users under 18 more than any other age group; users value connections more when they are also present on other platforms, indicating complementarity.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jun 1, 04:00

**Background**: Network effects refer to the increase in a product's utility as more people use it, a concept central to digital platforms. Local network effects focus on the value generated by specific social connections rather than the overall user base. Incentive‑compatible online choice experiments ensure participants reveal true valuations by linking payments to their choices, reducing hypothetical bias. Tie strength captures the intensity and resources embedded in a relationship, which varies across platforms and demographics.

## [AI Behavioral Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13323) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces a foundational framework for 'AI Behavioral Science,' describing how to assess AI behavior, use AI to study human behavior, and analyze the socioeconomic impacts of human‑AI interactions. As a nascent interdisciplinary field proposed by leading scholars, it offers a unified lens for evaluating opaque AI systems, advancing social‑science methods with AI, and anticipating the broader economic and political consequences of widespread human‑AI integration. It outlines three perspectives: (1) adapting social‑science tools to measure AI biases and heuristics; (2) employing AI techniques such as simulation and prediction to infer human behavior; and (3) examining the economic and political outcomes of increasingly intertwined human‑AI systems.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jun 1, 04:00

**Background**: AI systems are becoming ubiquitous, proprietary, and opaque, making it difficult to understand their behavior using traditional engineering metrics. Behavioral science has long developed methods for observing, measuring, and inferring human actions in complex social settings. By bridging these disciplines, AI Behavioral Science seeks to apply those methods to AI while also using AI to enhance the study of human behavior and societal impacts.
