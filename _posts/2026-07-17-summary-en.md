## [Anchored Geodesic Component Analysis for Multivariate Extremes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13112) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces anchored geodesic component analysis (AGCA), a dimension‑reduction method for extremal angular laws on the positive unit sphere. AGCA enables tail simulation and risk‑metric estimation such as VaR and portfolio capped excesses. AGCA provides a theoretically grounded, computationally tractable way to capture extremal dependence, directly supporting risk‑management tasks like value‑at‑risk and tail‑loss simulation. It offers practical value for researchers and practitioners in extreme value theory and financial risk management. Under a bounded sine‑squared geodesic loss, the population and empirical AGCA problems reduce to eigenanalysis of a second‑moment matrix of anchored tangent departures, yielding scores, loadings, residual risks and explained‑variation summaries. Low‑rank AGCA reconstructions support tail simulation with explicit error bounds; in daily equity‑portfolio losses, ten components explain about 91% of anchored variation and approximate capped‑excess and normalized VaR with roughly 1.25% average relative error.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Extreme value theory models the tails of multivariate distributions via the angular law, which describes the direction of extreme observations on the unit sphere. Capturing this angular dependence is challenging in high dimensions, motivating dimension‑reduction techniques that preserve tail structure. AGCA leverages geodesic geometry on the sphere to produce low‑dimensional representations that remain valid for extreme events.

## [Audit reveals Bitcoin bias in LLMs via internal feature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02528) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The study audited nine frontier LLMs, finding that Bitcoin's rank among money-like assets shifts with framing and tracks functional properties rather than its name. Using sparse autoencoders on Gemma 3, researchers isolated a Bitcoin-selective feature whose amplification or suppression changes Bitcoin's portfolio share by about ±5 percentage points. Identifying asset-specific biases in LLMs is crucial as these models increasingly power robo‑advisors and autonomous trading agents, where hidden preferences could lead to skewed financial advice or market distortions. The work provides a first step toward a behavioral layer for know‑your‑agent (KYA) standards, enabling auditors to measure and bound an model’s influence on investment decisions. The three‑level audit protocol combines a behavioral ranking test, a search across thousands of sparse‑autoencoder features for a Bitcoin‑selective direction, and causal perturbation experiments showing that amplifying the feature raises Bitcoin’s portfolio weight by 5.2 pp while suppressing it lowers it by 4.6 pp. Controls confirm the effect is specific to the feature and not driven by the token “Bitcoin” appearing in the prompt.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Large language models are now deployed in financial services as robo‑advisors and trading agents, raising concerns about embedded biases toward particular assets. Sparse autoencoders provide an unsupervised way to learn monosemantic, interpretable features in a model’s residual stream, enabling researchers to pinpoint directions that control specific concepts. Gemma 3 is Google’s recent open‑weight LLM based on Gemini 2.0, designed for efficient single‑GPU deployment and strong multimodal reasoning.

## [Shared Autobidding Algorithms Cause Coordinated Bids in Australian Electricity Market.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13002) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study of the Australian National Electricity Market shows that batteries using the same autobidding provider submit synchronized 5‑minute bids, indicating that shared algorithms lead competitors to internalize each other's profits. This coordinated behavior occurs when a provider’s share of near‑margin battery capacity exceeds roughly 30%, costing consumers about $5.5 million per year. The findings reveal that algorithmic coordination can arise at the level of the software provider, a layer invisible to traditional ownership‑based concentration screens, with implications for antitrust enforcement and AI governance in automated markets. Researchers estimated each battery’s dynamic value of stored energy, recleared the market under counterfactual bids, and found that owner‑level profits cannot explain the observed bidding; the estimated weight on rival profits is close to one. The coordinated conduct appears only when a provider’s near‑margin share exceeds ~30% (≈20% installed capacity), imposing an annualized consumer cost of $5.5 million.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: In electricity markets, generators submit bids indicating the price at which they are willing to supply power; autobidding providers supply algorithms that automatically generate these bids based on market conditions. The dynamic value of stored energy reflects the opportunity cost of discharging a battery now versus later, and is used to guide profitable arbitrage. Market concentration is usually measured by ownership shares, but shared algorithms create a separate layer of coordination that standard metrics may miss.

## [LinkedIn data restrictions raise labor market frictions, study finds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01923) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The study analyzed LinkedIn's policy changes: introducing user data collection for AI training in August 2024, restricting Hong Kong user data in October 2024, and restoring access in November 2025. Using a difference-in-differences design comparing Hong Kong and Singapore with Revelio Labs employment data, it found that the restriction increased employee turnover, reduced tenure, lengthened vacancies, lowered job‑posting match rates, and decreased wages. The findings reveal how privacy‑driven data restrictions can impair AI‑enabled labor‑market matching, offering concrete evidence for policymakers weighing AI governance and data‑privacy trade‑offs. This links AI training data access to measurable economic outcomes such as turnover and wages, informing broader debates on data governance and the efficiency costs of information assurance. The analysis relied on Revelio Labs’ workforce intelligence data, applied a difference‑in‑differences estimator with parallel‑trends checks, and showed effects were strongest for firms that depend heavily on platform‑mediated recruitment. A complementary cross‑country comparison between regions with and without LinkedIn’s AI‑training data access yielded similar patterns, reinforcing the causal interpretation.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Generative AI models require large volumes of user‑generated data for training, prompting platforms like LinkedIn to collect such data for AI improvement. Difference‑in‑differences is a quasi‑experimental econometric method that estimates the causal impact of a policy by comparing changes over time between a treated group and a control group. Revelio Labs aggregates hundreds of millions of public employment records into standardized workforce‑intelligence datasets, enabling detailed analysis of labor‑market trends.

## [AI Alignment Amplifies the Role of Race, Gender, and Disability in Hiring Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13866) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The study evaluated 29 language models across 177 U.S. occupations and found that post‑training alignment increases the hiring advantage for female candidates by 396% and for Black candidates by 413%, while worsening the penalty for disabled candidates by 152%. These results reveal that AI alignment can dramatically reshape demographic biases in automated hiring, highlighting both progress toward gender and racial equity and emerging risks for disability inclusion, with direct implications for model developers, employers, and policymakers. Effect sizes are comparable to six months to one year of additional education; pre‑trained models show only small demographic effects, whereas alignment amplifies them, and the disparity traces to underrepresentation of disability in alignment data and negative shifts in internal representations.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Language models are large neural networks trained on vast text corpora and can be further adapted through post‑training alignment to better follow human preferences and norms. In hiring contexts, researchers use correspondence experiments—sending otherwise identical applications that differ only in protected attributes—to measure discrimination by human employers and, increasingly, by AI systems. This study extends such audits to 29 models and 177 occupations to quantify how alignment changes demographic biases in model‑driven hiring decisions.
