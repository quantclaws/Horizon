This practice raises serious privacy risks because biometric data and device fingerprints can be combined to track individuals across services, undermining trust in age‑verification systems and highlighting the need for stronger data protection or zero‑knowledge alternatives. The study found that Yoti shares data with a range of brokers listed on page 12 of the report, and that the real‑time API creates a per‑query link between a user event and each broker, enabling live tracking.

Third‑party data brokers can aggregate such biometric and fingerprint data to build detailed profiles, which has prompted calls for privacy‑preserving alternatives such as zero‑knowledge proofs.

## [: Generative AI Exposure Measured via Two-Stage LLM Pipeline in U.S. Job Postings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23159) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Using a nationwide dataset of U.S. job postings, the authors built a two-stage large language model pipeline to measure generative AI exposure at the posting level and track its dynamics over time. They then decomposed aggregate exposure changes into hiring reallocation (52%) and within‑job redesign (39.5%), showing how firms adjust labor demand. The study reveals that labor‑market adjustment to generative AI involves both cross‑job reallocation and within‑job task redesign, with hiring shifts dominating early effects and task redesign growing over time. This nuanced view helps policymakers and businesses anticipate workforce transformation and design targeted reskilling initiatives. The two‑stage LLM pipeline first extracts task descriptions from each job posting and then scores how much generative AI can perform or assist those tasks, producing a posting‑level exposure score that varies over time. Decomposition shows hiring reallocation explains 52% of exposure decline, within‑job redesign 39.5%, and occupational composition accounts for about 90% of the change attributable to observable job characteristics.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · May 25, 04:00

**Background**: Generative AI is widely anticipated to reshape work, yet most studies have examined which occupations are exposed or whether exposed jobs decline, overlooking how firms reallocate hiring or redesign tasks. Job postings offer a timely, granular view of labor demand across all sectors, enabling measurement of exposure changes as they happen. Large language models have become effective tools for extracting and classifying textual content, allowing researchers to automate task identification from unstructured postings. This paper combines these elements to build a dynamic, LLM‑based exposure metric and analyze its two margins of adjustment.

## [: Strategic Coercion Within Alliances: Greenland Sovereignty as AI Stress Test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22841) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper simulates the Greenland sovereignty crisis using eight frontier LLMs as agents in three geopolitical games, collecting data from 3,604 completed games and 108,120 action observations to infer each model's structural utility parameters via inverse game theory. By providing a structural benchmark for LLM behavior in strategic coercion scenarios, the study bridges AI/ML and political science, revealing how model origins influence power‑weight profiles and escalation tendencies, which is relevant for AI safety and international policy. All eight models became more escalatory under coercion framing, with four‑action escalation rising from 10.7% to 28.6%; Chinese‑origin models showed distinct power‑weight profiles from Western ones when playing the U.S., and only DeepSeek V3.2 achieved a peaceful U.S. acquisition in a stable five‑round playbook, occurring in just 1.9% of clean games.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · May 25, 04:00

**Background**: Frontier large language models are state‑of‑the‑art neural networks trained on massive text corpora, capable of complex language generation and reasoning tasks. Inverse game theory works backward from observed outcomes to estimate the underlying utility functions and preferences of players in a game. A critical mass tipping point game models how a minority group’s actions can trigger a self‑sustaining cascade once a threshold of participation is reached, often used to study norm enforcement in alliances.
