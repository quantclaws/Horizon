## [:Isomorphic Dynamic Programs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22076) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces order isomorphisms to relate dynamic programs, showing that optimality properties transfer between isomorphic formulations. It applies this to Epstein–Zin preferences with time‑preference shocks, proves equivalence between multiplicative Kreps–Porteus and risk‑sensitive preferences, and demonstrates a two‑order‑of‑magnitude accuracy gain in a multisector real business cycle model. By providing a rigorous isomorphism framework, the work enables transfer of known optimality results across different dynamic‑programming formulations, enriching both economic theory and computational practice. This cross‑disciplinary link can improve the accuracy and solvability of macroeconomic models that rely on recursive utility and preference specifications. Using conjugacy methods from dynamical systems theory, the paper constructs order isomorphisms between the Bellman operators of different dynamic programs. This yields a sharp optimality condition for Epstein–Zin preferences with time‑preference shocks, shows multiplicative Kreps–Porteus preferences are isomorphic to risk‑sensitive preferences, and yields a two‑order‑of‑magnitude accuracy gain in a multisector real business cycle model.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · May 22, 04:00

**Background**: Dynamic programming is a method for solving sequential decision problems by breaking them into simpler subproblems and using Bellman equations to compute value functions. An order isomorphism is a bijective, monotone mapping between two partially ordered sets that preserves the order structure, allowing properties such as optimality to be transferred when the sets are linked by such a map. Epstein–Zin preferences generalize expected utility by separating risk aversion from intertemporal elasticity of substitution, while Kreps–Porteus and risk‑sensitive preferences are specific recursive utility forms; real business cycle (RBC) models simulate macroeconomic fluctuations using technology shocks and often rely on numerical approximation of value functions.

## [:Imperfect Commitment in Maximal Extractable Value Auctions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22667) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper models Ethereum block builders who defect with probability ε and, when defecting, replicate a type‑specific fraction γ(τ) of the winning MEV opportunity. Searchers anticipate this and choose between a risky first‑price bid and a safe deterrence bid, leading to a piecewise equilibrium that is analyzed both theoretically and empirically using the libmev dataset. By quantifying how builder commitment flaws affect auction revenue and surplus extraction, the study shows that credible MEV auctions need both proper auction design and limits on builders’ ex‑post use of bid information. This informs protocol designers and highlights which MEV types are most vulnerable to builder defection. The model assumes a builder defects with probability ε and, upon defection, captures γ(τ) of the winner’s MEV; searchers bid either a first‑price bid v or a deterrence bid b = γ(τ)v that makes defection unprofitable, yielding a piecewise equilibrium whose cost depends on replicability and competition. Empirically, γ(τ) is estimated from right‑tail bribe plateaus in the libmev dataset, revealing high competitiveness for sandwich opportunities but significant exposed surplus for naked arbitrage and liquidations.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · May 22, 04:00

**Background**: In Ethereum, block builders run sealed auctions where searchers submit bundles of transactions and pay bribes to have their bundles included; the builder then selects the winning bundle and constructs the block. Nothing in the protocol forces the builder to honor the auction outcome after seeing the bundles, creating a commitment problem. A first‑price auction awards the highest bidder, while a deterrence bid is set to make any post‑auction exploitation unprofitable. The libmev dataset records historical bribes and bundle outcomes, enabling measurement of how much MEV a builder could replicate after defection.
