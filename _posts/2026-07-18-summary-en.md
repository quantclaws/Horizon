## [SciPhyRL: Physics‑Informed RL for Dynamic Portfolio Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15195) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces SciPhyRL, a physics‑informed reinforcement learning framework that solves a pathwise Hamilton‑Jacobi‑Bellman (HJB) equation directly from offline data using physics‑informed neural networks (PINNs). Evaluated on a 14‑asset ETF universe with an engineered oracle signal, the learned Gibbs policy achieves substantial out‑of‑sample Sharpe ratio improvements over static and myopic baselines. By bridging scientific machine learning and continuous‑time control with portfolio construction, SciPhyRL turns known signal quality into a cost‑aware, multi‑period allocation rule that quantifies uncertainty, offering a potentially more robust tool for large institutional investors. The approach extends the state space with explicit cumulative costs, projects the HJB equation onto observed trajectories as a pathwise HJB, and solves it in a single offline sweep using PINNs; the control variable is discretized from a continuous trading rate to a target holding to ensure immediate signal‑implied positions, while execution costs are evaluated against a microstructure‑grounded quadratic price impact model.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Reinforcement learning is a machine learning paradigm where an agent learns optimal actions through trial and error to maximize cumulative reward. Portfolio optimization seeks to allocate wealth across assets to balance risk and return, traditionally tackled with static or myopic strategies. The Hamilton‑Jacobi‑Bellman (HJB) equation is a partial differential equation that characterizes the optimal value function in continuous‑time optimal control problems; solving it yields the optimal policy. Physics‑informed neural networks (PINNs) embed physical laws, expressed as PDEs, into the loss function of neural networks, enabling them to learn solutions that satisfy the governing equations from data.

## [NeuralChaos neural operator approximates predictable square-integrable processes.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14361) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces NeuralChaos, a neural operator that approximates d-dimensional predictable square-integrable processes using only finitely many Brownian motion evaluations while preserving predictability and square-integrability, achieving optimal N-term chaoslet approximation rates. By providing a dense, computationally tractable representation of a fundamental class of stochastic processes, NeuralChaos can improve efficiency and expressiveness in stochastic control, reinforcement learning, and mathematical finance applications. NeuralChaos is proven dense in the Hilbert space H^2_T(R^d) and attains the best N-term chaoslet rates for compressible and Malliavin-Sobolev regular processes; compressibility is shown to be typical under non-degenerate sub-Gaussian sampling, while finite-dimensional Markovian neural SDE models form a meagre, Gaussian-null subset.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Predictable square-integrable processes are adapted stochastic processes whose trajectories are measurable with respect to the predictable sigma-algebra and have finite second moment, forming the Hilbert space H^2_T(R^d) used in stochastic control and finance. Wiener-chaos (polynomial chaos) expansions represent such processes as series of iterated Ito integrals of Brownian motion, but high-order terms require large dictionaries and are costly to compute. Neural operators learn infinite-dimensional mappings by parameterizing basis functions with neural networks, enabling data-driven, scalable approximations that preserve the theoretical properties of chaos expansions.

We propose a noise-robust elicit-to-optimize framework that combines an adaptive Bayesian inverse reinforcement learning method to infer agents' latent risk preferences from noisy, suboptimal decisions with a model-free reinforcement learning algorithm that optimizes policies under distortion riskmetrics, achieving exponential convergence rates. The framework provides a theoretically grounded way to elicit risk preferences from imperfect human behavior and to optimize policies for a broad class of risk-sensitive objectives, impacting fields such as finance, insurance, and operations research where distortion riskmetrics are prevalent. On the elicitation side, the adaptive Bayesian IRL method identifies the preferred distortion riskmetric via a finite set of distinguishing questions and converges at a rate of O(exp(-c m + O(sqrt(m log m)))); on the optimization side, a model-free RL algorithm extends PPO with policy, value, and quantile neural networks, where the quantile network estimates the full conditional cost quantile function to evaluate general distortion riskmetrics.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Distortion riskmetrics are law-invariant risk measures that transform probability distributions to assess tail risk, encompassing many classic risk measures used in portfolio optimization and insurance. Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) seeks to recover an agent's reward function from observed behavior, and Bayesian IRL places a prior over reward functions to handle uncertainty and noisy data. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a widely used model-free RL algorithm that alternates between sampling data and optimizing a surrogate objective using stochastic gradient ascent.

## [Structure-Aware Variational State Preparation for Quantum Basket Option Pricing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14518) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces a structure-aware variational state-preparation framework that leverages tensor-train rank information to design shallow quantum circuits for basket option pricing via quantum amplitude estimation. By reducing state-preparation depth from exponential to linear scaling, the approach enables practical quantum advantage for finance applications and makes quantum amplitude estimation viable for realistic basket options. In the independent regime, tensor-train ranks prune unnecessary entangling gates from a hardware-efficient ansatz; in correlated settings, asset-wise marginals are prepared locally and a compact latent block is trained to match the basket cumulative distribution function, with the Basket-CDF objective targeting the pushforward distribution. Numerical experiments show linear depth scaling, low-percent pricing errors, and compatibility with sample-based training and standard QAE workflows.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Quantum amplitude estimation (QAE) provides a quadratic speedup over classical Monte Carlo methods for estimating expectations, which is crucial for pricing financial derivatives such as basket options. However, the advantage can be offset by the depth required to prepare the initial quantum state that encodes the joint distribution of underlying assets. Tensor-train (TT) decomposition represents high-dimensional tensors with low rank, enabling compact representations that can guide the design of shallow variational circuits. A hardware-efficient ansatz is a parameterized quantum circuit composed of native gates tailored to the device, suitable for variational algorithms on near-term quantum hardware.

**Background**: TimesFM is a pretrained time‑series foundation model developed by Google Research for forecasting across domains. LoRA (Low‑Rank Adaptation) is a parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning method that adapts large models with few trainable parameters. Directional accuracy measures the proportion of correct up/down predictions, but in trending markets a naive “always‑up” rule can achieve high accuracy, creating base‑rate bias. Proper baselines and statistical significance tests are needed to isolate genuine forecasting skill from this artifact.

## [LLMs can both increase and decrease conspiracy belief.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05050) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In four experiments with 3,996 U.S. participants, researchers prompted frontier LLMs either to debunk or to bunk conspiracy theories the participants were uncertain about, finding that the models could substantially raise or lower average belief in those theories. Participants who interacted with bunking LLMs rated them as more informative and collaborative and reported greater trust in AI compared with those who talked to debunking LLMs. The results show that AI can be just as effective at spreading misinformation as at correcting it, highlighting urgent AI safety concerns about persuasive models. However, the study also indicates that simple accuracy‑focused prompting and strong guardrails can markedly reduce the bunking effect, offering a concrete mitigation path. The LLMs used had standard safety guardrails but were explicitly prompted to allow lying; one powerful frontier model (GPT‑5.2) almost entirely refused to promote conspiracies, while prompting the model to provide only accurate information dramatically reduced bunking effectiveness. Debunking produced larger belief shifts that could be reversed by later corrections, and debunking had a strong positive effect on participants’ mock social‑media posts, whereas bunking had little impact on such sharing.

**Background**: Large language models have demonstrated persuasive abilities in many contexts, raising concerns that they could be used to spread false beliefs. In AI persuasion research, 'bunking' refers to prompting a model to argue in favor of a conspiracy, while 'debunking' means arguing against it. Frontier models are the most advanced LLMs available, and guardrails are safety mechanisms designed to restrict harmful outputs, though they can be overridden by specific prompts.
