## [Hacker News Revisits 1998 W3C Principle: Cool URIs Don't Change](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Hacker News discussion revived attention to Tim Berners-Lee's 1998 W3C principle 'Cool URIs Don't Change,' with users sharing real-world examples of broken links from Microsoft, NSF, and even W3C's own accessibility page, highlighting ongoing failures in URI persistence. The principle remains foundational for web architecture, as link rot undermines trust, accessibility, and the long-term usability of digital resources, affecting developers, publishers, and end users who rely on stable references. Commenters noted that while modern tools like WordPress slug redirects and HTTP 301/302 mitigations help, failures persist due to reorganizations, neglect, or sites going offline — exemplified by a 404 on an NSF.gov link from 1998 and W3C's own wai-aria-practices page lacking a stable URI.

hackernews · Klaster_1 · Aug 9, 14:32 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49231809)

**Background**: The 'Cool URIs Don't Change' principle, introduced by Tim Berners-Lee in 1998, argues that URIs should remain permanent to ensure long-term linkability and trust in the web. It emphasizes that servers can decouple URIs from physical file paths through techniques like content negotiation and redirects, allowing resources to move without breaking links. The principle is a cornerstone of web architecture, advocating for deliberate URI design to prevent link rot.
