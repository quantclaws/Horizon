## [: Option pricing under non-Markovian stochastic volatility models: A deep signature approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15237) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In August 2025, the authors introduced a deep signature approach that prices options under non-Markovian stochastic volatility models by representing rough stochastic differential equations as linear or nonlinear combinations of time-extended Brownian motion signatures, thereby converting them to classical SDEs and proving algorithm convergence. This method bridges rough path theory and machine learning, offering the first provably convergent deep learning algorithm for option pricing in path-dependent volatility settings, which could improve pricing accuracy and computational efficiency in quantitative finance. The approach represents the volatility process via signature expansions of time-extended Brownian motion, learns the signature coefficients with a neural network, and establishes convergence of the learned approximation to the true option price; numerical tests show effectiveness for both Markovian (e.g., Heston) and non-Markovian (e.g., rough Heston) models.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jun 2, 04:00

**Background**: In non-Markovian stochastic volatility models, the future volatility depends on the entire past path of the process, making traditional PDE-based option pricing intractable because the price is a functional of the whole history. Rough path theory provides a framework to handle such irregular signals by encoding their information in signature expansions, which are infinite collections of iterated integrals. By expressing the volatility as a linear or nonlinear combination of time-extended Brownian motion signatures, the rough SDE can be rewritten as a classical SDE, enabling the use of standard analytical and numerical tools.

## [: Strategic Preemption in AGI Race: Suicide Region and Shared Risk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07526) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper models the AGI race as a continuous‑time preemption game with shared catastrophic externalities, introducing a “suicide region” where rational actors deploy AGI despite negative social value. It shows that higher catastrophe costs widen this region and that private liability and prize‑sharing can close it. By formalizing the strategic incentives that drive unsafe AGI deployment, the work offers a clear theoretical basis for policy interventions such as liability rules and prize‑sharing schemes. This helps policymakers understand why simply increasing the perceived risk of catastrophe may not deter the race and what complementary measures are needed. The model’s equilibrium indifference condition cancels the risk term because the catastrophe cost appears in both players’ payoffs, creating a suicide region that expands as ruin costs rise. Welfare loss is measured against a social planner’s benchmark, and the paper shows that imposing private liability raises deployment costs while prize‑sharing reduces the first‑mover advantage.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jun 2, 04:00

**Background**: In game theory, a preemption game captures situations where players compete to take an action first, often modeled in continuous time to avoid simultaneity issues. When the action carries a shared catastrophic risk—such as a potential AGI‑induced existential disaster—the cost of that risk enters each player’s payoff. The paper shows that because the risk cost is common, it drops out of the indifference condition, leading to a “suicide region” where rational players still choose to act despite negative expected value.

## [Model-Free ARIES-SEDEx Pipeline Extracts Risk-Neutral Densities from Short-Dated Options.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22792) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces a two‑step model‑free pipeline: first, the Arbitrage Removal Iterative Executable Strategy (ARIES) cleans bid‑ask quotes of executable static arbitrage; second, the Smooth Entropic Density EXtraction (SEDEx) derives a smooth, entropic risk‑neutral density from the cleaned panel. The method is validated on synthetic Heston simulations and short‑dated SPX option data ranging from a few hours to one week before expiry. By directly working with bid‑ask quotes, the pipeline mitigates the impact of wide spreads and stale quotes that plague short‑dated options near expiry, making density extraction feasible where traditional mid‑quote methods fail. The resulting densities enable more accurate implied‑volatility smiles and improve pricing, hedging, and trading strategies for market practitioners and researchers. ARIES formulates an iterative executable strategy that enforces market‑depth constraints while removing static arbitrage opportunities at the quoted bid and ask levels. SEDEx then maximizes entropy subject to smoothness penalties and bid‑ask inequalities, producing a density that is both smooth and maximally non‑informative; the algorithm runs quickly and has been shown to generate robust densities across various market conditions, including periods around macroeconomic announcements.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jun 2, 04:00

**Background**: Risk‑neutral density (RND) encapsulates the market’s probability distribution of future asset prices implied by option prices, and is essential for pricing exotic derivatives and assessing market sentiment. For short‑dated options, as expiration nears, option premia shrink and bid‑ask spreads can become large relative to the price, while stale or asynchronous quotes may introduce static arbitrage violations that destabilize conventional extraction techniques. Model‑free approaches avoid assuming a specific parametric price process, instead treating the observed bid‑ask quotes as direct market constraints to recover the RND robustly.

## [: The Privacy Subsidy: Kyle's λ under Noise-Perturbed Order-Flow Observation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15746) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper derives a closed-form linear Kyle equilibrium for cryptocurrency order flow perturbed by independent Gaussian privacy noise, showing that the price‑impact coefficient and informed‑trader strategy scale reciprocally with the privacy parameter. It then identifies a per‑period transfer from the liquidity provider pool to traders—the 'privacy subsidy'—which equals the break‑even fee a privacy‑preserving exchange must charge. By linking the privacy subsidy to the established loss‑versus‑rebalancing (LVR) concept, the work provides a concrete formula for setting fees in shielded AMMs and other privacy‑preserving crypto exchanges. This informs protocol designers about the necessary fee to break even when injecting privacy noise, bridging market microstructure theory with practical DeFi design. Both the price‑impact coefficient and the informed‑trader strategy rescale by reciprocal factors of the privacy parameter, leaving their product invariant. A welfare decomposition yields a closed‑form per‑period transfer from the LP pool to traders—the privacy subsidy—which serves as the single‑period analogue of Loss‑Versus‑Rebalancing (LVR).

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jun 2, 04:00

**Background**: The Kyle model describes how a market maker sets prices based on noisy order flow, with a price‑impact parameter λ reflecting adverse selection from informed traders. In privacy‑preserving crypto exchanges, independent Gaussian noise is added to the observed order flow to hide individual trades, altering the signal the market maker sees. Automated market makers (AMMs) such as Uniswap suffer from loss‑versus‑rebalancing (LVR), the adverse‑selection cost incurred when stale prices are exploited by arbitrageurs. The paper shows that the privacy subsidy derived under noise‑perturbed Kyle equilibrium is analytically equivalent to the per‑period LVR, providing a unified view of adverse selection in both traditional and privacy‑enhanced AMMs.
