## [Researchers release new county-level consumption dataset from 350 million credit cards.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08759) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The paper introduces a monthly county-level consumption dataset built from spending data on over 350 million credit cards in the Federal Reserve's Y-14M reports, validates it against PCE, and demonstrates heterogeneous spending responses to monetary policy shocks across income groups. The dataset provides a high‑frequency, granular measure of consumption that closely tracks official PCE, enabling researchers to study distributional effects of monetary policy that were previously infeasible with aggregate data. The dataset covers over 3,000 U.S. counties from 2014 onward, explains 92% of the variation in national monthly adjusted PCE growth, and shows low‑income counties experience larger spending declines than high‑income counties after monetary policy shocks.

**Background**: The Federal Reserve's Y-14M reports collect confidential, monthly credit‑card‑level data from large banks, which researchers can aggregate to measure spending. Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) is the primary gauge of consumer spending in the U.S. national accounts. Heterogeneous Agent New Keynesian (HANK) models extend standard New Keynesian frameworks by incorporating income and wealth heterogeneity, allowing analysis of distributional macroeconomic effects.

## [Volatility in Prediction Markets: A Structural Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08199) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The authors develop and estimate a volatility model for binary prediction markets that combines a Wright-Fisher deadline-resolution component with a Glosten-Milgrom order-flow component, and validate it on a large panel of Kalshi contracts. This structural model outperforms standard ARCH/GARCH benchmarks, offering better volatility forecasts for derivatives pricing, market making, and risk management in prediction markets, and advances financial econometrics and market microstructure research. The model integrates Wright-Fisher drift (capturing forced resolution of binary uncertainty) and Glosten-Milgrom spread dynamics (capturing volatility from informed trading); using Kalshi data, structural variables dominate plain ARCH/GARCH, and adding residual GARCH yields the best forecasts, with volatility highest near 0.5 price and rising as deadlines approach.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Prediction markets trade contracts whose prices represent the probability of a future binary event, with payoffs of either 0 or 1 and a known expiration deadline. Unlike standard assets, these prices are bounded between 0 and 1, so traditional return‑based volatility models such as ARCH/GARCH are not directly applicable. The Wright‑Fisher process, originally from population genetics, describes how allele frequencies drift toward fixation over discrete generations and can be adapted to model the forced resolution of binary uncertainty as a deadline approaches. The Glosten‑Milgrom model captures how informed trading widens bid‑ask spreads and generates volatility through order‑flow asymmetry. Combining these two mechanisms yields a structural volatility framework suited to prediction‑market contracts.

## [Volatility‑Scaled SDF from S&P 500 Options Predicts Equity Premium.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08500) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper proposes a volatility‑scaled stochastic discount factor (SDF) derived solely from S&P 500 option prices, revealing a non‑monotonic SDF with a hump that evolves into a W‑shape as maturity increases, and shows that the resulting equity premium forecasts outperform existing benchmarks out‑of‑sample. This approach provides a forward‑looking, model‑free measure of market risk preferences that can improve asset‑allocation and risk‑management decisions, and offers a new empirical bridge between option markets and the equity premium puzzle. The SDF is scaled by time‑varying volatility extracted from option implied volatilities, exhibits a distinctive hump on the shallow put side that sharpens with longer maturity, and its theoretical shape is justified under constant market price of risk in a stochastic volatility framework.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: A stochastic discount factor (SDF) prices assets by discounting future payoffs with the market’s stochastic risk aversion; it is central to asset pricing theory. Option prices contain risk‑neutral expectations that can be inverted to recover the SDF, especially when using broad indices like the S&P 500. The equity premium is the excess return of stocks over risk‑free bonds, and the Martin bounds provide a model‑free lower bound on this premium using option data. The paper improves upon these bounds by scaling the SDF with time‑varying volatility to capture forward‑looking risk preferences.

## [Stablecoin Activity in Austria Shows Global Integration and Institutional Dominance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08524) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers used Austria's crypto-asset service provider registry to trace on-chain transactions of Bitcoin, Ether, USDC, and USDT through May 2025, finding that Austrian CASPs handled about USD 30 billion with external counterparties, dominated by a few institutions in value but many retail-like actors in count, and that stablecoin flows reacted differently during the Terra‑Luna, FTX, and SVB shocks. The findings reveal how crypto‑asset markets transmit risk to the national economy and show that stablecoins do not act as a uniform safe haven during stress, which is crucial for financial stability monitoring and informs the design of central bank digital currencies. The study separates retail‑like from institutionally mediated flows, shows that around three major shocks the two groups respond via different mechanisms, and notes that SVB‑related patterns align with USDC’s two‑tiered redemption model, a detail invisible in aggregate data.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Crypto‑asset service providers (CASPs) are firms that offer services such as exchanges, custody, or transfers for digital assets and are subject to anti‑money‑laundering rules. In Austria, CASPs must register with the financial regulator under the national AML act, which incorporates the EU’s MiCA framework, allowing regulators to collect on‑chain address data. Blockchain transactions are pseudonymous, lacking built‑in geographic tags, making it hard to locate users without external identifiers like a regulator‑maintained registry.

## [Optimistic inflow forecasts distort hydrothermal dispatch and market outcomes in Brazil](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00504) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The study shows that persistent optimistic inflow‑forecast bias reduces water values, increases first‑stage hydro discharge, lowers reservoir storage, and delays thermal commitment in Brazil’s hydrothermal power system. Using official Brazilian data and an SDDP experiment, the authors find that biased forecasts lead to lower reservoir levels, delayed dry‑season thermal dispatch, sharper price spikes, higher reliability risk, and higher expected operating costs. The results reveal that inflow‑forecast bias is not just a statistical error but a source of operational inefficiency, reliability risk, and distorted market incentives in hydro‑dominated systems. These insights are relevant to other regions with large hydro shares and growing reliance on energy storage, where accurate inflow forecasts are crucial for efficient dispatch and contract design. Analytically, optimistic bias weakly reduces water values and weakly increases first‑stage hydro discharge relative to the unbiased optimum, leading to lower storage and postponed thermal commitment. Empirically, policies trained under biased forecasts produce lower reservoir levels, delayed dry‑season thermal dispatch, sharper spot‑price peaks, higher reliability risk, and higher expected operating costs, while increasing price‑quantity risk for hydropower producers and reducing their willingness to contract.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: In hydro‑dominant power systems, centralized hydrothermal planning models use inflow forecasts to compute the water value—the marginal opportunity cost of stored water—and to determine generation schedules and spot prices. Stochastic Dual Dynamic Programming (SDDP) is a common method for solving multi‑stage stochastic optimization problems under uncertainty, where the first‑stage decision corresponds to the immediate hydro discharge plan. Optimism bias refers to the tendency to overestimate future inflows, which can skew these calculations and distort operational decisions.

## [Governing Generative AI Across Financial Institutions: An SR 26-2-Compatible Framework for Generative AI Risk Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04103) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper proposes the Generative AI Control Framework (GAICF), an SR 26-2-compatible governance approach for managing risks of generative AI in financial workflows. It fills a regulatory gap by providing a practical governance model that aligns emerging generative AI use with the updated SR 26-2 model risk management expectations, helping banks mitigate risks that could affect financial decisions. GAICF translates core model risk management principles into a layered control structure for generative AI applications that operate outside formal model boundaries but remain embedded in regulated banking processes, addressing uses such as monitoring interpretation, policy analysis, and adverse-action language drafting.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: SR 26-2, issued April 17, 2026 by the Federal Reserve, OCC, and FDIC, replaces SR 11-7 and introduces a more risk‑based, materiality‑sensitive supervisory framework for model risk management. Generative and agentic AI are explicitly excluded from SR 26-2, creating a governance challenge for financial institutions. Although generative AI does not directly estimate credit risk, its outputs can materially affect the control environment by influencing how regulated decisions are explained, challenged, documented, and governed.

## [Fair Document Valuation in LLM Summaries via Cluster Shapley](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23842) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces Cluster Shapley, an efficient approximation of Shapley values that groups semantically similar documents using LLM embeddings and computes values at the cluster level, providing formal error bounds and showing superior performance over Monte Carlo sampling and Kernel SHAP on Amazon product review data. By enabling fair attribution of value to source documents, the method addresses compensation concerns in AI‑driven content ecosystems and can be applied broadly to any LLM‑based summarization system. Cluster Shapley uses LLM embeddings to form document clusters, computes Shapley values per cluster, and provides bounds on both approximation error and induced revenue‑attribution error; it is agnostic to the underlying LLM, summarization process, and evaluation procedure.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: The Shapley value from cooperative game theory offers a principled way to fairly distribute gains among contributors, but its exact computation grows exponentially with the number of documents, making it infeasible for large‑scale LLM summarization. Approximation techniques such as Monte Carlo sampling and Kernel SHAP trade off accuracy for efficiency, often performing poorly in LLM settings. Cluster Shapley improves this trade‑off by first clustering similar documents and then applying Shapley computation at the cluster level, yielding provable error bounds.
