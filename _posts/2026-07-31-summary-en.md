## [Authors propose duality-corrected contracts for volatility control.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27039) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces a general class of contracts parameterized by a function ψ that ensures the contract is revealing for the agent and without loss of generality for the principal. It provides two natural specifications: a forcing‑type contract inspired by BSDEs and a duality‑corrected contract that resolves the duality gap when Assumption 2.3 of Cvitanić et al. (2018) does not hold. By identifying a hidden assumption in prior optimal contract designs and proposing a more general, duality‑corrected contract class, the paper removes a source of potential sub‑optimality in volatility‑control problems. This advancement improves the robustness of optimal incentives in continuous‑time principal‑agent settings, with direct implications for financial contract design and stochastic control applications. The approach requires ψ to satisfy measurability and monotonicity conditions that guarantee the resulting contract is revealing and principal‑optimal without loss of generality. One specification leads to a standard BSDE (forcing‑type contract), while the other leads to a 2BSDE that corrects the duality gap when Assumption 2.3 of Cvitanić et al. (2018) fails.

rss · arXiv Quantitative Finance · Jul 30, 04:00

**Background**: In continuous‑time principal‑agent problems, the principal designs contracts to incentivize the agent who can control both the drift and volatility of the underlying process. Earlier work by Cvitanić, Possamaï and Touzi (2018) solved this using dynamic programming and second‑order backward stochastic differential equations (2BSDEs), but relied on Assumption 2.3 that the volatility‑control problem admits a certain martingale representation. More recent BSDE‑based approaches reformulate the problem as a contractible‑volatility sub‑game, and the present paper shows that dropping Assumption 2.3 requires a more general contract class that can be expressed via either a BSDE or a 2BSDE depending on the chosen ψ.

The paper introduces a physics-informed neural estimator that learns to clean empirical cross-covariance matrices, improving out-of-sample prediction and reducing portfolio tracking error on U.S. equity data. By overcoming the stationarity and bounded-spectrum assumptions of traditional shrinkage methods, the approach provides more robust covariance cleaning under realistic, non-stationary market conditions, delivering tangible benefits for portfolio replication and risk management. The estimator represents the cleaned cross-covariance in the empirical singular-vector basis and learns a nonlinear mapping from empirical singular values and marginal projections to cleaned singular values, retaining stability where analytical shrinkage deteriorates as the universe size grows.
